## Mission
Explain A25 in understandable terms: what it is, why it exists, how it works, what it can and cannot do, and what a human should consider before using it. Compare it responsibly with mainstream products so readers can make their own informed choice, including choosing not to use A25. Attention and discovery may help people find the project, but this team is **not for money**.

## Navigation
- `onboarding/`: team orientation
- `lead/`: responsibilities, rules, recovery memory and notes
- `explainers/`: plain-language descriptions and practical use cases
- `comparisons/`: sourced comparisons with mainstream alternatives
- `claims-and-evidence/`: technical claim provenance and review status
- `questions/`: human questions, concerns and understandable answers
- `requests/incoming/` and `requests/responses/`: cross-team fact checks and replies
- `public-materials/`: user-facing introductions and educational copy
- `project-sidecars/`: links to the actual project and its source history

## Responsibility boundary
Sales does not invent features, perform technical certification, set policy or speak for another product's provider. Workshop, Security and Research own their technical findings; Archives preserves originals. Public-facing descriptions must say what is implemented, proposed, experimental or unknown. Changes to owner-approved positions require owner authorization.

## Standing principles
This is public education, not monetization. No prices, revenue targets, conversion quotas, affiliate promotions or pressure tactics. Be clear about costs or requirements that actually exist, but do not invent a business model. Preserve owner seals and original evidence. Distinguish REFERENCE, HISTORICAL CLAIM, VERIFIED, ADOPTED, SUPERSEDED and UNKNOWN. No misleading security, privacy, capability or performance claims. No new branches, deployment or live changes are authorized by this documentation.
